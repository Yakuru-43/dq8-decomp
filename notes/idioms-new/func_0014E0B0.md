## A split block in an if-without-else stops CW from laying it cold; one signed `/ 2^n` makes it cold
Sign: an `if (x != 0) { fixup; } secondBlock;` where the original lays the
**second** block inline (the if test falls through to it) and the fixup cold
after it, with the fixup's tail branching **back** to the second block
(`b <second>; sw $vN,off` in the delay slot); mine puts the fixup inline and
skips the second block with a forward `beqz` (CLASS structural, ~19/124, every
word present, blocks interchanged). Look for a `bltz`-style split inside your
fixup that the original doesn't have — the original's fixup is **one** basic
block ending in `bgez <join>; sra …` (a signed division by 2^n), mine was the
hand-written ternary `(d >= 0) ? (d >> 4) : ((d + 15) >> 4)` = two blocks +
`bltz`. The extra blocks change CW's hot/cold decision and force it inline.
Fix: (1) write the divide as the plain signed division `n = d / 16;` on an
`s32` (see idioms.md "plain `bgez`, not `bgezl`: signed r / 2^n") — the
single-block expansion flips the layout by itself; (2) if the size then comes
out 4 B short and the tail lacks a `b` back to a shared `b <epi>; nop` stub,
**remove** the explicit `return;` from the last if's arm: the arm's store then
lands in the delay slot of a `b` back to the stub both arms share
(`b <stub>; sw $aN,off`). Also check the struct for a 4-byte hole where CW
loads a higher offset than your field (here 0x30 before limit at 0x34).
