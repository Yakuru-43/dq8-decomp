## The wall

Same allocation wall as sibling `func_00211770` (see its note), but the
entry-chain trick partially defeats it:

- Single mutated variable (`cursor += 8`) — **always** folds: CW keeps a
  base copy (`daddu $s1, $a0`) and emits `addiu $a0, $s1, k*8` per call,
  cursor register dies, `nop` in the jal delay slots. score 99–102.
- **Five separate variables** `entry0..entry4; entry(k+1) = entry(k) + 8`
  — defeats the fold: CW emits the `addiu $s0, $s0, 8` chain and
  `daddu $a0, $s0` like the original. Still 468 vs 452 bytes, score=104,
  because the **scheduling** differs:
  - original: `jal / addiu $s0 (delay) / daddu $a0, $s0 / daddu $sN, $v0`;
    mine: `jal / nop / addiu $s0 / daddu $sN, $v0 / ... daddu $a0, $s0`
    (mine sets the arg in the *next* jal's delay slot instead).
  - prologue: original interleaves `daddu $s6, $a1` between the sq saves
    (+0x14) and keeps the `daddu $s0, $a0` in the guard delay slot; mine
    saves all seven registers first, then copies the args.
  - `< 4` test: original `slti $at, $s6, 4` + `beqz` + `addiu $s5, 0x12`
    in the delay slot; mine `slti $v0, $s1, 4` + `bnel` (constants path
    fall-through vs mine branch-target, arg copy in the delay slot).
  - mine materialises `&bufC0` once into `$s6` and reuses it; original
    re-issues `addiu $a0, $sp, 0x80/0xC0` at every call.
