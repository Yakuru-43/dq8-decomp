## What doesn't

Block order. Original: `[tests][return-0 branch][main body +0x510..0x630]
[0x64 handler at +0x634][shared return-0 block +0x67C][return-1 tail]`.
All four C shapes I tried — `if/else`, `if(){guards} if(){main} handler`,
`A-first` and `main-first` orders — make mwcc place the `0x64` handler
immediately after the tests (+0x50), before the main body, cascading ~105
words of offsets. This is the same rule P14/P15/P25 hit and it is still
unwritten down: **when the CFG has several guard exits plus shared tails,
what decides which block mwcc keeps in fall-through position?**

One real type finding on the way: the `D_003E19B0`/`D_003E19A8`/`D_003E19AC`
bytes are read with `lb` (sign-extend) — declare them `s8`, not `u8`
(`lbu` vs `lb` is a 1-word diff the relocation mask hides; try_match's
MATCH is not the authority — verify.py caught it).

Sharpened diagnosis (the three scheduler decisions C cannot express here):

1. **Return-stub tail-merge.** Original: the three `return 1;` paths share
   ONE physical stub (+0x168 `b E1; addiu v0,1`): len>0 falls into it,
   len<=0 and the recs!=0 bnel-block bounce back to it (`b .L0046362C`);
   the three return-0 paths share the `.67C` `daddu v0,zero` + `.680`
   `b → lq; ld ra` pair (guard-1's `b` even targets the mid-block `.680`
   with `daddu v0,0` in its own delay slot). My early returns get PRIVATE
   2-word stubs in source-local positions (`b E1; daddu v0,0` inline at
   +0x64 instead of `bne → .67C`) — mwcc tail-merged the original's but
   not mine, regardless of nesting shape (tested `!= 1` guard, `== 1`
   nested no-else, `== 1` + `else return 0`, single trailing return).
2. **bgtzl vs blez.** Original keeps the len>0 block out-of-line behind the
   len<=0 fall-through, with the (dead) `lb $a0,D_003E19A8` in the bgtzl
   delay slot; mine inverts to `blez s0 + nop` and inlines len>0.
3. Consequently the 0x64 handler + shared-stub tail sit 9 words late.
